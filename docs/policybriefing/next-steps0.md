# Next Steps: From Model Experiments to Real Welsh Policy

The three-region model provides a framework for exploring how policy-related
shocks may affect Wales, the rest of the UK and the UK economy as a whole.
The current simulations are exploratory and model-conditional. They are not
official forecasts or validated estimates of the impact of specific Welsh
Government programmes.

The next stage is to connect the model more closely to real policy design and
available evidence.

## Refine

Map a clearly defined policy instrument into the model.

This means specifying the policy mechanism, who is affected, the geographical
coverage, the timing of the intervention and how it is funded. The model
representation should reflect the policy’s actual design as closely as the
available structure allows.

## Validate

Compare the model’s assumptions and simulated responses with programme evidence,
fiscal costs and regional data.

Relevant evidence could include programme objectives and eligibility, spending
and take-up, implementation timelines, evaluation findings, and data on
regional or sectoral outcomes. This step would help identify which assumptions
are supported by evidence and where uncertainty remains.

## Apply

Use the refined and evidence-informed model to compare feasible policy
scenarios.

Comparisons should make the assumptions explicit, including fiscal costs and
funding. Outcomes could include Welsh GDP per capita, the Wales–UK gap, UK-wide
output and the timing of economic adjustment. Results should be presented as
model-based scenarios, with sensitivity analysis and clear limitations.

## Where policy collaboration could help

Welsh Government expertise could help identify:

- policy priorities and relevant instruments;
- programme design, delivery and costing;
- available regional data and evaluation evidence.

A useful first exercise would be to select one concrete policy question, agree
how the intervention should be represented, and identify the evidence needed
to assess the model’s assumptions.
