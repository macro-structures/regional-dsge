# Regional DSGE

Understanding the regional economy does not require persuasive narratives or ad-hoc explanations.
It requires a structural framework.
Regional economy is analysed through a structural DSGE framework. The model is built on internally consistent behavioural relationships and equilibrium conditions.
The framework is evaluated against three stringent criteria:
1.	Existence and uniqueness (or stability) of equilibrium solutions
—	ensuring that the model defines a coherent economic environment in which trade-offs are well specified and internally consistent.
2.	The ability to pass Bayesian-type evaluation
—	ensuring that the implied data-generating process is statistically plausible and not ruled out by observed macroeconomic evidence.
3.	Empirical credibility, assessed through indirect inference (II) testing against observed data
—	ensuring that the dynamic behaviour generated by the model resembles key features of the real economy.
The purpose is not prediction, but to stress-test mechanisms and identify regions of fragility in policy design.
While the model may suggest that regional productivity gap within the UK reflects structural features and initial endowments, this does not imply economic determinism. Instead, the framework clarifies which types of structural reforms can meaningfully alter long-run outcomes, and which cannot.
